mandatory check-in call must happen between **21 September and 2 October**,
and it releases the second half of the grant.

## What is not working, in order of what it costs

The rubric is 100 points: technical execution 30, innovation 20, impact 20,
user experience 10, documentation 10, cloud delivery and reproducibility 10.
The COOL award weights *verified COOL on Graviton* at 30% and the Agentic
Vision award weights *substantive OpenCV 5 and agent integration* at 30% and
*orchestration and appropriate autonomy* at 25%.

### 1. Nothing exists on AWS, and the person who owns it has not started

No account, no budget alarm, no Dockerfile, no `infra/`, no arm64 build, no
COOL AMI subscription. That is the whole of the cloud delivery score, the 30%
"verified COOL integration" of the COOL award, and the demo endpoint the
rules require. It is also what the check-in call will ask about, and the
window opens in 19 days.

The proposal assigns AWS, the COOL benchmark and the agent to Alex. The repo
has 26 commits, all by Uriel; Alex has 0 commits, 0 reviews, 0 comments in
two weeks. Both PRs were merged unreviewed. The critical path sits entirely
with the person who has not touched the repo. This is a planning fact, not a
complaint, and it needs a decision this week, not in October.

### 2. There is no product surface at all

No API, no page, no way for a judge to pick a slide and watch it run. User
experience, documentation/presentation and cloud delivery are 30 points on
which we currently score zero, and "functional web endpoint or live demo" is
a hard requirement.

### 3. The number a user gets is wrong by 44%, and the vision is why

With expert counts the agent lands within 3.7% of the true density. With the
real pipeline the mean absolute density error per slide is **43.9%**. The
agent's decisions barely change between the two (469 vs 470 fields); the
answer does. The detector is the bottleneck: candidate recall is 0.88 and no
classifier can find a parasite the top-hat never proposed, and precision on
fields with fewer than 10 parasites is 0.575.

The reference paper on this exact dataset (Yang et al., IEEE JBHI 2020, same
150 patients, candidate screening plus a small CNN) reports 92.6%
sensitivity and 94.3% precision at the candidate-patch level in five-fold
patient cross-validation. We are at 0.70 / 0.84 at the object level. The gap
is real and it is where the impact lives.

### 4. Specificity has never been measured

Every one of the 150 NLM patients is infected. The proposal promises
"sensitivity and specificity per patient", the impact story is *screening*,
and a screening tool that calls clean slides positive is useless. With
sparse-field precision at 0.575 the detector will find parasites on a
negative slide; we do not know how many, or whether the stopping rule for
negatives (200 white cells, zero parasites) can ever fire.

NLM publishes two more thick-smear sets from the same group:

| archive | patients | size |
|---|---|---|
| `NIH-NLM-ThickBloodSmearsU.zip` | uninfected | 2.45 GB |
| `NIH-NLM-ThickBloodSmearsPV.zip` | 150 *P. vivax* | 4.98 GB |

The first gives specificity. The second gives the "other species" failure
case the proposal promised. Tanzania adds 1 071 uninfected thick images at
image level. `data/download_nlm_extra.py` fetches them.

### 5. The stopping rule optimises the wrong thing

"Stop when the 95% interval is within 20% of the estimate" needs about 380
parasites, so only 15 of 50 slides ever reach it and the agent runs out of
slide on 24 of 50. A clinician does not need a number to 20%; they need a
decision: positive or negative, which density band (under 1 000, 1 000 to
10 000, 10 000 to 100 000, over 100 000/µL, the hyperparasitaemia line), and
on day 3, any parasites at all. Stop when the interval sits inside one band,
or on one side of the threshold that matters for this patient. That is what
"appropriate autonomy" means, it is what a technician actually does, and the
replay harness measures it in one second.

### 6. The agent is a rule, and the proposal promised a model

`policy.py` lists `Bedrock: the real agent (to write)`. The rule-based
policy is the right control and it must stay, but a judge scoring
"substantive OpenCV 5 and agent integration" and "orchestration" will ask
where the agent is. What a model can do that thresholds cannot: reason over
a slide whose fields disagree, handle borderline quality, decide *what to ask
a human and why*, and write the technician's report from the trace. Build it
behind the same `Policy` interface, record every call so evaluation replays
free of charge, and measure it against the rule. If it does not beat the
rule, the report says so and the rule ships.

### 7. The COOL story is untested, but the profile is on our side

Measured today, one field on a laptop, 8 threads (`scratchpad`, to move into
`bench/`):

| stage | median s | share |
|---|---|---|
| read the JPEG | 0.21 | 6% |
| assess_field | 0.28 | 8% |
| find disc + normalise stain | 0.99 | 28% |
| white cells | 0.29 | 8% |
| parasite candidates (top-hat) | 1.25 | 36% |
| patch CNN through cv.dnn | 0.29 | 8% |
| **total** | **3.5** | |

85% of the time is classical `imgproc`, exactly what COOL accelerates, so a
whole-pipeline speedup is plausible and not just a microbenchmark. But two
stages are slow for no good reason: a Lab colour transfer on a 2 680² crop
should not take a second, and neither should one top-hat. That is almost
certainly float64 NumPy work, and a judge who profiles will see it. Fix it
before timing anything, and make the benchmark time the real
`count_field`, not ten isolated calls.

### 8. Stage 3 has nothing to register

NLM fields do not overlap, so the promised "line up views so nothing is
counted twice" cannot be built as written. What can: use ORB and a
homography to *verify that `move_stage()` produced a new field* and refuse
to count a repeat, with a test where the stage is stuck. That keeps the
promise in the form the data allows and it is a failure-handling feature,
which the Agentic rubric scores.

### 9. The held-out set has been looked at too often

Eight epochs against twenty, hybrid against CNN, three thresholds: six
choices were made by reading the held-out numbers. Those 50 patients are now
a validation set and the report must call them that. Tanzania is the only
untouched test data and stays that way. The U and PV sets arrive frozen: run
once, at the end, with the pipeline fixed.

### 10. Process

No CI, so tests run only on one laptop. PRs merged by their author. The local
working branch was behind `develop`. Cheap fixes: GitHub Actions on every PR,
including an arm64 build; each PR reviewed by the other person; a fixed
weekly call.

- per-patient sensitivity ≥ 0.95 on infected and specificity ≥ 0.90 on the
  uninfected set, stated with the number of patients;

  the decision it settled;

  whole pipeline, with the build line that proves which library ran.
