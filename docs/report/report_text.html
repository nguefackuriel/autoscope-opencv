AutoScope is a screening aid, not a diagnostic device. It has not been clinically validated. All images are public, retrospective, de-identified research material.

1The problem

Microscopy gives a number. A rapid test gives yes or no.

The WHO World malaria report 2025 counts 282 million cases and 610 000 deaths in 2024. 94% of cases and 95% of deaths were in the African Region. Three quarters of those deaths were children under five.

Rapid diagnostic tests carry most of the field load. Two things limit them. pfhrp2 gene deletions, found in 42 countries, remove the commonest rapid-test target from the parasite. Artemisinin partial resistance is detected by counting parasites on day 0, day 2 and day 3. A rapid test cannot count.

The clinical quantity is parasites per microlitre. A technician obtains it at an eyepiece by counting parasites against 200 white blood cells on a thick film. It is slow. The precision depends on how many cells were counted before the technician stopped. Where there are few microscopists, the count is skipped.

Who it is for

A district hospital or health centre with a microscope, a technician, and no slide scanner. A phone camera is held to the eyepiece. The fields are uploaded. The count comes back with a confidence interval and the crops it was made from. A person signs the result.

2What AutoScope does

Six OpenCV stages produce the counts. An agent decides how many fields to read. A fixed function, not the agent, turns the counts into a call.

The loop

A field arrives. OpenCV finds the lit disc, scores focus, evenness and debris, normalises the stain, segments white cells, proposes parasite candidates with a top-hat, and scores each candidate with a small convolutional network through cv.dnn. The running totals go to the agent with the current density estimate and its Poisson interval. The agent picks one of five moves. When it stops, density.decide() reads the totals and returns the call. The trace of every move, with the agent's reason for each, is what the technician reviews.

The five moves. src/autoscope/agent/moves.py.
MoveWhen
count_then_moveThe field is readable. Count it and advance.
refocusThe focus score is low. Step the focus and look again.
skipDebris, a bubble or an edge. Do not count it.
stopThe interval is tight enough, or the protocol's cell count is reached.
flag_for_humanThe evidence will not settle. Escalate.

3The OpenCV 5 pipeline

Six stages, all imgproc and dnn, pinned to opencv-python 5.0.0.93.

Stage 1. Field of view

A phone through an eyepiece gives a bright disc on a black surround. fov.detect thresholds the image, closes it with an elliptical kernel and keeps the largest component. Every later stage works inside the disc.

Stage 2. Field quality

Three numbers per field: focus, from the variance of a Laplacian inside the disc; evenness, from the illumination field; and a debris fraction. These are the inputs to refocus and skip.

Stage 3. Stain normalisation

Giemsa staining varies between laboratories more than parasites vary between patients. The pipeline works in Lab colour space and uses the a−b channel as the stain map. This separates nuclear purple from background at any stain intensity.

Stage 4. White cells

White cells are the denominator of every density. Nuclei are segmented from the stain map and separated with a distance transform and connected components.

Stage 5. Parasite candidates and the patch network

A morphological top-hat on the smoothed stain map leaves small dark purple objects. Local maxima above a threshold, outside the white-cell mask, are the candidates. Candidate recall against all annotated parasites is $candidate_recall. Each candidate is cut to a 48 px patch and scored by a small CNN, exported to ONNX and run through cv.dnn. The same binary runs on the laptop and on Graviton. The operating threshold is $threshold, chosen on 20 calibration patients by the rule closest to zero count bias.

Stage 6. Density and interval

density() converts parasites per white cell to parasites per microlitre at the WHO convention of 8 000 leucocytes/µL. The confidence interval is an exact Poisson interval. At a few parasites in 200 cells, the normal approximation is too narrow.

4The agent

The agent chooses where to look and when to stop. It does not choose the diagnosis.

The agent returns a structured move. When the move is stop, it also returns its own opinion of the call. That opinion is recorded and not used. The call comes from density.decide(), the same function that serves the rule-based policies. So the comparison between an agent and a rule is a comparison of stopping points only.

Replay without cost

Every model call is recorded to a JSONL cache, keyed on the model, the effort setting, the system prompt, the tool definitions and the observation. $cache_calls calls across $cache_models models ship with the repository. A changed prompt changes the key. A cache miss during replay raises instead of calling the model. The comparison re-runs with no AWS account.

5What is already known, and what is not

Automated malaria microscopy is a solved research problem at the field level. What is not solved is the slide.

The detector. Yang et al. is the paper that goes with this dataset: a smartphone-based detector on the same 150 patients, reporting 92.6% sensitivity and 94.3% precision at the candidate level.1 Everything measured here is read against that. Our contribution is not a better detector. Our detector is deliberately a small CNN on top of classical morphology, because it has to run through cv.dnn on an Arm instance beside five other OpenCV stages.

The protocol. How many fields to read is not a modelling question, it is a WHO one: count parasites against 200 white cells, or read 100 fields before calling a slide negative.2 That protocol is a fixed budget. It spends the same effort on a slide swarming with parasites as on an empty one. Nothing in the literature we found treats "how much of this slide is enough" as a decision to be made per slide and measured.

What is new here. Three things, and each one is a measurement rather than a claim.

  1. The stopping decision is separated from the diagnosis and measured on its own. Every policy in Section 7 reads the same fields with the same counter and the same decide(); only the stopping point changes. That is what makes an agent comparable to a rule at all, and it is why the honest answer about a fixed eight fields could be found rather than argued about.
  2. Fifteen models through one loop, replayable for nothing. $cache_calls recorded calls ship with the repository. The finding is that model size does not predict anything and willingness to stop does: $agent_never_stop of the $agent_model_arms never chose stop once. That is not visible on any model card and costs one cheap run to check.
  3. A defect in a shipped accelerated OpenCV, diagnosed to the call and worked around. Section 9. The function benchmark says 1.59 times faster; the pipeline says 3.8 times slower; the thread sweep says why. Function-level benchmarks are how hardware gets chosen, and here is a case where they point the wrong way.

Observability. $tf_log_groups CloudWatch log groups, $log_retention_days-day retention: the API, the Step Functions state machine and the Batch jobs. The state machine logs at ALL with execution data on, so every field read, every agent move and the reason it gave are in the log, not only the final answer. Each slide also writes one DynamoDB row per move, which is what the demo page reads back. An alarm fires on more than $alarm_sessions sessions started in five minutes, and two budgets, $$$budget_monthly a month overall and $$$budget_bedrock for Bedrock, email on 80% forecast and 100% actual.

Security. $tf_roles IAM roles, one per component, each scoped to the bucket, table, state machine or function it needs by ARN. All $tf_buckets S3 buckets block public access and are encrypted at rest; the demo page is served through CloudFront, not from a public bucket. The API has no write path to anything but the uploads prefix and is throttled to 10 requests a second with a burst of 20, so a loose link cannot run up a bill. Deployments use GitHub OIDC with no long-lived keys. No patient-identifying data exists: the slides are public de-identified research images.

Human control. The agent has no way to issue a diagnosis. Its moves are count, refocus, skip, stop and flag_for_human, and the call always comes from decide() on the counts. When the agent stops it may state its own opinion of the call; that opinion is recorded and never used, and the two agreed on all $arm_bedrock_found_agent_agreed of the slides where it gave one. A slide it cannot settle goes to a person: $arm_bedrock_found_escalated of $agent_patients slides with the shipped counter. Every result carries the field images and the per-move reasons that produced it, so a reviewer checks the work rather than taking the number.

Qwen3-Coder-Next skipped $arm_qwen3_coder_next_skips times and reached $arm_qwen3_coder_next_found_mape% error.

Where the fields go is the difference. The fixed policy reads eight fields on every slide whether the parasites are everywhere or almost absent: on the ten densest slides it reads $alloc_fixed_dense and on the ten sparsest $alloc_fixed_sparse, and its field count barely tracks the slide (r = $alloc_fixed_r against expert density). The agent spends the same budget unevenly. It reads $alloc_agent_dense fields on the ten densest slides and $alloc_agent_sparse on the ten sparsest, from $alloc_agent_min to $alloc_agent_max across the set, r = $alloc_agent_r. It stops after one field when the interval is already inside a band and keeps reading when it is not.

So the measured claim is this one, and not a larger one: at the same cost and the same error, the agent puts its effort where the evidence is thin, states a reason for every move, and the reason is on the demo page next to the field it was made on. Against an oracle counter it is behind both the rule ($arm_bedrock_truth_mape% against $arm_decision_truth_mape%) and the fixed policy. Stopping earlier costs accuracy on exact counts.

8Failure modes

Figures in docs/figures/. Catalogue in docs/results-parasites.md.

  1. Sparse slides. With few parasites, false positives dominate. This sets the $lod_per_ul/µL limit of detection and the $floor/µL noise floor.
  2. Platelets and stain debris. The commonest false positive is a small round purple object. The patch network removes most. The rest are in docs/figures/parasite-false-positives-sparse-fields.png.
  3. Other species. F1 $pvf1 on P. vivax, with no usable confidence signal.
  4. Soft fields. On a virtual stage, refocus can only choose among captured focus planes. On hardware it is untested.

The workaround

The library runs the morphology on one core, so the pipeline hands it work one core can do. parasites.py splits the image into horizontal bands, one per thread, each band carrying a halo as wide as the kernel radius times the number of chained passes, and runs the top-hat and the dilation band by band. Thirty pixels of halo for the top-hat, seven for the dilation. The result is checked against the unsplit call before it is timed, so a wrong answer cannot be reported as a fast one. It is forty lines behind AUTOSCOPE_TILED_MORPH=1, off by default.

With it on, the candidate stage falls from $cool_c8g_cool_stages_cand s to $cool_tiled_candidates s and the COOL image reads $tiled_sph slides an hour against the pip wheel's $stock_sph.

10Limits and responsible use

Screening aid

A person reviews every output. The trace, the crops and the interval are shown so the technician can disagree. AutoScope has not been clinically validated and has not been run on a clinical sample. It must not be used to make a treatment decision.

Virtual stage

No microscope or motorised stage was available. refocus and the stage moves select among fields already captured. The comparison between policies holds, because every policy sees the same fields. Stage control on hardware is untested.

Data

Development and most evaluation use NLM/LHNCBC thick films from one collection. The Tanzanian set is the only transfer evidence. AUC $tzauc there is well below the within-collection figures. A deployment would need local calibration slides. The threshold-choosing script is in the repository for that purpose.

Species

Falciparum only. On P. vivax the detector reads F1 $pvf1 and its confidence score does not indicate failure. It cannot be used where vivax circulates.

Test-set discipline

The 50 held-out patients were fixed before any parasite work and never changed. The uninfected frozen half, the Tanzanian set and the vivax set were each scored once. Re-scoring requires an explicit flag and a statement in this report. The threshold was chosen on 20 calibration patients by a stated rule.

The guards, and where each one is

Every limit is outside the model. A policy that keeps answering "one more field" stops at the state machine, not when it decides to.

11What would make the detector stronger

Measured on the cached candidate scores. Nothing here is shipped: each item changes counts, which would invalidate every recorded model call and every frozen figure above.

What limits it

The density error tracks the per-slide spread of the found-to-true parasite ratio, not F1. The spread is false alarms, not recall: across the 50 held-out slides the false-alarm rate per true parasite varies with a coefficient of variation of $sc_fa_cv%, recall with $sc_rec_cv%. Nine slides carry $sc_nine_share% of that variance. The other $sc_rest_slides read $sc_rest_err% error with $sc_rest_cv% spread. Six of the nine are sparse slides that over-count on detections scored just above the threshold: rods, streaks and clumps. Three are dense slides with soft focus whose parasites score near zero. Every counter trained for this project fails on the same nine.

The soft count also removes the cancellation described in Section 7: with an exact white cell count the shipped count gets worse and the soft count does not. It costs a few dozen lines and no training. It is the first thing to build.

Context around the patch

The parasites the judge rejects are fainter than the false alarms it accepts. That is the judge's own axis. Features of the surroundings, neighbours, white cells, field debris, do not separate the two error sets. A wider patch or a second-stage classifier on context is not worth building.

A single-stage detector

The requirement is per-slide spread under 20% at bias under 5% on the 20 calibration patients, with the sparse and the dense failure modes both inside ±25%. F1 does not predict this. The box detectors trained on Colab need their validation split separated from the calibration patients before their numbers can be compared, then one GPU run scored on density.
