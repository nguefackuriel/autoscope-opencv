AutoScope

AutoScope is a screening aid, not a diagnostic device. It has not been clinically validated. Every development and evaluation image is retrospective, de-identified, public research material.

1The problem, and who this is for

Microscopy is still the reference method for malaria, and the thing it gives you that a rapid test cannot is a number.

The WHO World malaria report 2025 counts 282 million cases and 610 000 deaths in 2024, with 94% of cases and 95% of deaths in the African Region and just over three quarters of those deaths in children under five. Rapid diagnostic tests carry most of the field load and they answer yes or no. Two things break that answer. pfhrp2 gene deletions, found in 42 countries as of 2024, make the commonest RDT target disappear from the parasite. And artemisinin partial resistance is detected by counting parasites on day 0, day 2 and day 3 and watching whether the count falls — a measurement an RDT cannot make at all.

So the quantity that matters is parasites per microlitre, and the way it is obtained is a technician at an eyepiece counting parasites against 200 white blood cells on a thick film. That is slow, it is tiring, and the count's precision is set by how many cells were counted before the technician stopped. Where there are few microscopists the work does not get slower; it gets skipped.

Who AutoScope is for

A district-hospital or health-centre laboratory that already has a microscope and a technician, and no budget for a slide scanner. The intended shape is a phone camera held to the eyepiece, the fields uploaded, the count and its confidence interval returned with the crops that produced it, and a person signing the result. The technician is not removed from the loop; the counting and the decision of when the count is precise enough are what move.

What we did not have

No microscope, no motorised stage, no clinical samples, and no institutional data access. Everything here is built and measured on the public NLM/LHNCBC thick-film sets plus a CC0 Tanzanian set, and the stage is a virtual one: the agent's moves select among real captured fields rather than driving real hardware. Section 9 states exactly what that does and does not license us to claim.

2What AutoScope does

Six OpenCV stages produce the numbers. An agent decides how many fields to spend getting them. A fixed function, not the agent, turns the numbers into a call.

The loop, in one paragraph

A field arrives. OpenCV finds the lit disc, scores focus, evenness and debris, normalises the stain, segments white cells, proposes parasite candidates with a top-hat and judges each one with a small convolutional network through cv.dnn. The running totals — parasites and white cells — go to the agent as a short observation together with the current density estimate and its exact Poisson interval. The agent picks one of five moves: count this field and move on, refocus, skip it, stop, or hand the slide to a person. When it stops, density.decide() reads the counts and returns the call. The trace of every move, with the reason the agent gave, is what the technician reviews.

The five moves

The agent's whole action space. Defined in src/autoscope/agent/moves.py.
MoveWhat it is for

3The OpenCV 5 pipeline

Six stages, all imgproc and dnn, pinned to opencv-python 5.0.0.93.

Stage 1 — the field of view

A phone through an eyepiece produces a bright disc on a black surround, and the surround is most of the frame and none of the slide. fov.detect thresholds, closes with an elliptical kernel and takes the largest component, so every later stage works on the lit area and the black border never enters a histogram or a denominator.

Stage 2 — is this field worth counting

Three numbers per field: a focus score from the variance of a Laplacian, measured inside the disc only; an evenness score from the illumination field; and a debris fraction. These are what make refocus and skip mean something — an agent that cannot tell a soft field from a sharp one has no reason to prefer one move over another.

Stage 3 — stain normalisation

Giemsa varies between laboratories more than parasites vary between patients. The pipeline works in Lab and uses the a−b opponent channel as the stain map, which is what makes the purple of a nucleus separable from the background regardless of how heavily the slide was stained.

Stage 4 — white cells, the denominator

Parasites per 200 white cells is what becomes a density, so an error here moves every number downstream. White cell nuclei are segmented from the stain map and separated with a distance transform and connected components.

Stage 5 — parasite candidates and the patch network

A morphological top-hat on the smoothed stain map removes the background and leaves small dark-purple objects; local maxima above a threshold, outside the white-cell exclusion mask, are the candidates. Candidate recall against every annotated parasite is $candidate_recall, which is the ceiling the classifier works under. Each candidate is cut to a 48 px patch and scored by a small CNN exported to ONNX and run through cv.dnn — the same binary on the laptop and on Graviton, which is why the cloud and local numbers agree. The operating threshold is $threshold, chosen on 20 calibration patients by the rule closest to zero count bias, never on the held-out set.

Stage 6 — density with an honest interval

density() converts parasites per white cell to parasites per microlitre using the WHO 8 000 leucocytes/µL convention, and attaches an exact Poisson confidence interval rather than a normal approximation, because at the counts that matter — a handful of parasites in 200 cells — the approximation is wrong in the direction that flatters us.

Why this is OpenCV and not incidental to it

Take OpenCV out and the project does not get slower, it stops existing: there are no field quality scores, so refocus and skip have no input; there are no counts, so there is no interval; and there is nothing for the agent to decide. The agent's entire observation is OpenCV output.

4The agent: what it decides, and what it never decides

The agent chooses where to look and when to stop. It never chooses the diagnosis.

This is a design decision with a measurement behind it, and it is the one that makes the comparison in Section 6 meaningful. The agent returns a structured move, and when that move is stop it also returns its own opinion of the call. That opinion is recorded and not used. The call comes from density.decide(), the same arithmetic that serves the rule-based arms, so what is being compared between an agent and a rule is the stopping point and nothing else. Trusting the model's self-reported call would have quietly turned a controlled comparison into a comparison of the rule's arithmetic against the model's opinion.

Recording the model's own call anyway buys a number we could not get otherwise: how often the model's self-assessment agrees with what its own counts actually say. A disagreement rate is a finding in either direction.

The full trace of every move on every slide is in eval/results/agent_eval.json, and the twelve rendered frames of one session — the real images, the detections circled, the interval narrowing, the move and its reason — are on the demo page and in docs/figures/session/.

Reproducible at zero cost

A full comparison is one model call per loop step, about 600 to 1 200 per arm, so a judge could not re-run it and neither could we without paying again. Every call is therefore recorded to a JSONL cache keyed on the model, the effort setting, the system prompt, the tool definitions and the observation. $cache_calls calls across $cache_models models ship with the repository in that cache; of the lookups in the published run, $agent_calls_cached were served from it and $agent_calls_live were live. Editing the prompt or the move definitions changes the key, so a changed prompt cannot silently replay decisions it never made, and a cache miss during replay raises rather than falling through to a live call. The comparison re-runs with no AWS account and no cost.

Cost discipline

A $$100 monthly budget alarm, Spot for every benchmark instance, and a trap that terminates the instance on any exit. That trap is not sufficient on its own — stopping the orchestrating shell skips it, and one c8g.8xlarge billed for fourteen minutes before we noticed — so each instance now also runs shutdown -h +90 on itself, with the launch set to terminate on shutdown.

The plan written on 2 September set seven numeric targets for 26 October. We met $n_met of $n_crit, and the two we missed are stated here rather than in a footnote.

The detector, on patients the fit never saw

$held_patients held-out NLM patients, $held_images images, $held_truth annotated parasites, at threshold $threshold: precision $prec, recall $rec, F1 $f1. Count bias $held_count_bias_pct%, and the parasite-per-white-cell ratio — the quantity that becomes the density — tracks the expert's with a Spearman correlation of $held_ratio_spearman and a bias of $held_ratio_bias_pct%.

This misses the 0.85 we set ourselves. The honest reading is that object-level F1 is the wrong target for this product and we should have noticed in September: what a laboratory acts on is the slide-level call and the density, and both are carried by the decision rule, which reads $sens sensitivity with none of the $dec_infected_slides infected slides called negative. An F1 of $f1 is good enough for that and not good enough for the claim we wrote down. Both are true and the second is the one we got wrong.

Specificity, on the frozen uninfected half

$neg_patients uninfected patients, $neg_fields fields, the $neg_which — material scored once, with nothing fitted on it. $neg_clean_fields_pct% of fields come back completely clean; pooled false parasites run at $neg_false_per_200 per 200 white cells, with a worst field of $neg_false_max.

At slide level: $dec_clean_called_positive of $dec_clean_slides clean slides were called positive. $dec_clean_to_person were escalated to a person. Because an escalation is not a negative, specificity as the plan defined it reads $spec and misses the 0.90 target. We are reporting both numbers because they say different things and only quoting one would be a choice about which to hide: the pipeline produces no false positive calls on clean slides, and it also cannot clear 40% of them without a human. For a screening aid whose output is reviewed by a person that is a defensible place to sit, but it is a cost — it is human time — and calling it 0.90 specificity would be false.

Density, and the limit of detection

On $density_slides slides the density is within ±25% of the expert count on $density_within_25% and within ±50% on $density_within_50%, mean absolute error $density_mae_pct%. By dilution, over $lod_repeats repeats, the 95%-sensitivity limit of detection is $lod_per_ul parasites/µL. The measured noise floor on clean slides is $floor/µL, and the rule will not call a slide positive below it.

The honest limit on the agent result

Run against an oracle counter instead of the shipped one, the agent's density error is worse than the rule's ($arm_bedrock_truth_mape% against $arm_decision_truth_mape%). Stopping earlier is free only because the detector is not yet good enough for stopping early to cost anything. If the detector improves, this comparison has to be re-run, and the agent may lose it.

Generalisation, each run once

Another laboratory. A CC0 Tanzanian set — different microscope, camera and stain — scored once at commit $tz_commit with the pipeline fixed: image-level AUC $tzauc over $tz_infected_images infected and $tz_uninfected_images uninfected images, with $tz_uninfected_at_zero% of uninfected images returning exactly zero parasites.

Another species. The falciparum detector on P. vivax, $pv_patients patients, $pv_images images, run once: F1 $pvf1 against $f1 on falciparum, with $pv_under10 patients under 10% recall. We had planned to ship a species warning based on the detector's own confidence. That is not possible: the median score on detections it gets right is $pv_score_matched and on the ones it gets wrong is $pv_score_unmatched. The score is slightly higher on the mistakes, so there is no threshold that separates them and no automatic warning we can honestly give. The limitation is stated on the demo page with the number instead.

7Where it fails

Four failure modes with a number attached to each. The figures are in docs/figures/ and the catalogue is in docs/results-parasites.md.

  1. Sparse fields. On slides with very few parasites the false-positive rate dominates the true-positive rate; this is what sets the $lod_per_ul/µL limit of detection and why the rule has a noise floor at $floor/µL rather than treating one detection as evidence.
  2. Platelets and stain debris. The dominant false positive is a small, round, purple object that is not a parasite. The patch network removes most of them; what survives is in docs/figures/parasite-false-positives-sparse-fields.png.
  3. Another species. F1 $pvf1 on P. vivax, with no usable confidence signal (Section 6).
  4. Soft fields. The focus score is what lets the agent refocus instead of counting a blurred field, but on a virtual stage refocus can only choose among the focus planes that were captured. On real hardware this stage is untested.

Six of the seven pipeline stages are faster or level on the COOL image. One, parasite candidates, is $cand_ratio× slower and swallows all of it. The ten tile cases could not say which call did that: they time a 5×5 kernel over uint8 at one megapixel, and the stage runs a 31×31 ellipse over float32 at twelve. So we added a case group at the real shapes and dtypes, and swept the thread count.

$sweep_rows
The candidate stage's real calls, 4032×3024. Medians of 10 runs, milliseconds.
callstock @32COOL @32 stock @1COOL @1 stock gainCOOL gain

Read the last two columns. Forced to a single thread the two images agree to within 6% on every call. The kernels are the same speed. The stock wheel then takes 15–25× back by spreading the work over the 32 cores; on the COOL image those four calls gain nothing, at any thread count.

cvtColor is the control, and it is the line that makes this a measurement rather than a guess: it parallelises on the COOL image exactly as well as on the stock wheel. TBB is working and the threads are there. What does not happen is that the HAL-intercepted filter and morphology paths use them. On a 1 MP tile that costs almost nothing, which is precisely why the microbenchmark reports $geo_cool× and the pipeline reports the opposite.

What we take from this, and what the COOL team can

For us it settles the question: we do not run on the Marketplace image as it stands. For the COOL team it is a bug report with a two-line reproduction. One limit, stated plainly: our pipeline leans unusually hard on large-kernel morphology, and a pipeline that did not would see the $geo_cool×. This is a finding about an interaction between one image and one workload, not a claim that the COOL image is slow.

Reproduce both halves with: 

GROUP=pipeline THREADS=0,1 COOL_AMI=<ami> bash bench/run_on_ec2.sh c8g.8xlarge c8g-cool 10

9Limits, and responsible use

The honest statement of what this is not.

It is a screening aid, not a diagnostic device

Every output is reviewed by a person, and the product surface is built around that: the trace, the crops and the interval are shown so a technician can disagree. AutoScope has not been clinically validated, has never been run on a clinical sample, and must not be used to make a treatment decision.

The stage is virtual, and that is load-bearing

refocus and the stage moves select among fields that were already captured. The agent's policy is measured honestly against the rules on exactly the same fields, so the comparison is sound — but the claim "this drives a microscope" is not one this work supports. Motorised-stage control is untested.

The data is not the deployment population

Development and most evaluation use NLM/LHNCBC thick films from one collection. The Tanzanian set is the only evidence of transfer to another laboratory, it was run once, and an AUC of $tzauc is a long way from the within-collection numbers. Any real deployment would need local calibration slides before it could be trusted, and the pipeline provides the threshold-choosing script for exactly that.

Species

Falciparum only. On P. vivax the detector reads F1 $pvf1, and — this is the part that matters for safety — its confidence score cannot tell you it is failing. A laboratory where vivax circulates cannot use this as built.

Test-set discipline

The 50 held-out patients were separated by the white-cell work and never moved. The uninfected frozen half, the Tanzanian set and the vivax set were each scored once, with the pipeline fixed; re-running any of them requires an explicit flag and would have to be declared. The threshold was chosen on 20 calibration patients by a stated rule. Where a number moved between drafts, both readings are in the repository with the reason.

Privacy and licence

All imagery is public, retrospective and de-identified: the NLM/LHNCBC thick-film sets and a CC0 Tanzanian set. No patient identifiers are processed, stored or transmitted. Uploaded fields in the demo go to a private bucket behind the API and are not published.
