AutoScope

Malaria thick-film screening. OpenCV 5 on Graviton, an agent deciding where to look.

Four real sessions read on AWS, one per density band, so you can watch the agent spend effort in proportion to the evidence: TF41_UT5 stops after 3 fields at 90,000/µL, TF101_233 takes 8 to settle at 8,600, TF22_C104P65 reads all 11 it has and still straddles a band boundary at 700, and TF150_R5 runs out of slide at 288 and goes to a person. All four are NLM held-out patients the fit never saw. Or leave the endpoint blank and “Replay a recorded session” reads sample-session.json from this page’s own folder, so the demo still works with nothing deployed and a judge can check it offline.

What the agent looked at

Twelve fields of patient TF101_233, in the order the agent read them. Green rings are parasites, blue are white cells. Both are count_field's own detections at the shipped threshold, drawn on the real capture. The running estimate and the agent's move are printed on each frame.

There is no slide being panned across here, and that is deliberate: NLM fields are separate captures minutes apart, not tiles of a mosaic. A sequence of fields is what a technician sees down an eyepiece.

Rejecting a detection recomputes the count and the density from the corrections. Nothing is sent anywhere from this page; the corrections are what a deployment would keep as training data.

Screening aid, not a diagnostic device. Every result is reviewed by a person. AutoScope detects P. falciparum and does not identify species. That is measured, not assumed: on 90 annotated P. vivax patients this detector reads F1 0.29, against 0.80 on falciparum, and its confidence score does not separate the detections it gets right from the ones it gets wrong, so there is no automatic warning to give you. A slide from a region where P. vivax circulates needs a human reader. All development used retrospective, de-identified research slides (NLM/LHNCBC, NIH) and nothing here has been clinically validated.
